\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Power line communication remains an unusual but useful communication technology because it reuses conductors that already exist for power delivery. This can reduce installation effort and cost, especially where separate communication wiring would be expensive, inconvenient, or technically difficult.

However, power lines are difficult communication channels. They are noisy, their impedance changes with connected loads and network conditions, and PLC systems require careful coupling, filtering, safety measures, and compliance with electromagnetic emission limits. Compared with dedicated data cables, optical fiber, or wireless systems, PLC often loses in speed, predictability, and reliability.

For this reason, PLC is unlikely to become a universal or mainstream communication solution. Its value lies in carefully selected cases. PLC should therefore be seen neither as obsolete nor as a replacement for other communication systems, but as a niche technology with persistent practical value wherever reusing power lines is the better engineering compromise.
